# PSAK 73 / IFRS 16: Right-of-Use (ROU) Assets & Lease Accounting Guide
**Case Study Focus:** PT Matahari Putra Prima Tbk (MPPA) · FY2020 Audited Financials & Strategy Turnaround

---

## 1. Executive Summary (The 30-Second Boardroom Pitch)

> **"PSAK 73 is the new lease accounting rule effective 1 January 2020 in Indonesia (identical to IFRS 16). It forced MPPA to treat all future mall rental contracts as debt on its balance sheet. Overnight, this added Rp 2.25 Trillion in lease liabilities and Rp 166.2 Billion in non-cash lease interest charges—making an already struggling hypermarket chain look financially insolvent right as COVID-19 mobility curbs emptied the shopping malls."**

---

## 2. What is PSAK 73? (Before vs. After)

* **Before 2020 (Old Operating Lease Rule):**
  Rent was treated as a normal monthly operating expense on the Income Statement. If MPPA rented an 8,000 m² hypermarket inside Lippo Mall Puri, rent was simply expensed when paid. **Zero debt appeared on the balance sheet.**
* **Starting 1 January 2020 (PSAK 73 / IFRS 16):**
  Accounting standards eliminated the distinction between operating and finance leases for lessees. Companies were mandated to capitalize **all future lease obligations**:
  * **Asset Side:** Record a **Right-of-Use (ROU) Asset** *(Aset Hak-Guna)* representing the economic value of using the mall spaces over the lease term (5–10+ years).
  * **Liability Side:** Record a **Lease Liability** *(Liabilitas Sewa)* representing the discounted present value of all future mandatory rent checks.

---

## 3. Why It Crippled MPPA Specifically in 2020

1. **Massive Balance Sheet Leverage on Weak Equity:**
   * MPPA operated **100 giant Hypermart stores** (averaging 5,000–8,000 m²).
   * PSAK 73 dumped **Rp 2,253.2 Billion (Rp 2.25 Trillion)** in lease liabilities onto MPPA's balance sheet (*AR2020 Note 19*).
   * Because 4 years of pandemic losses had shrunk Stockholders’ Equity to just **Rp 184.7 Billion**, MPPA's debt ratios exploded overnight, scaring commercial banks (BNI credit lines) and threatening covenant breach (Debt-to-Equity ceiling of 250%).
2. **The Profit-Killing Split (+126% Net Finance Cost Surge):**
   * Rent was no longer recorded as normal rent. It was split into:
     1. **ROU Depreciation** in OpEx (**Rp 329.6 Billion** / year).
     2. **Lease Finance Interest** in Net Finance Costs (**Rp 166.2 Billion** / year).
   * Although CEO Elliot Dickson cut store operating losses by **+Rp 55.8 Billion** in 2020, the **Rp 166.2 Billion lease interest charge** wiped out all gains, widening MPPA's pretax loss from `-Rp 314.6B` to `-Rp 407.7B`.
3. **The Pandemic Cash Trap:**
   * When government PSBB lockdowns closed malls, foot traffic collapsed by 50%+, and sales dropped 22% (Rp 8.65T → Rp 6.75T).
   * But **PSAK 73 lease obligations were locked in by legal contracts**. MPPA was legally bleeding cash on thousands of square meters of empty suburban floor space.

## 5. How Our Turnaround Strategy Fixes This

### Strategic Action: Downsizing 45 Hypermarkets & Subleasing Backrooms
Under **PSAK 73 Paragraph 44–46 (Lease Modification & Derecognition)**:
When MPPA downsizes an anchor hypermarket from 7,000 m² to 2,500 m², it renegotiates the contract to permanently eliminate the surplus lease liability:

| Account Title | Debit (Rp Billion) | Credit (Rp Billion) | Strategic Mechanism |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Dr. Lease Liabilities** *(Derecognition)* | **Rp 450.0 B** | | Wipes excess future rental debt off balance sheet |
| **Dr. Accumulated Depreciation – ROU Assets** | **Rp 120.0 B** | | Removes accumulated depreciation on eliminated space |
| **Cr. Right-of-Use Assets** *(Gross Cost)* | | **Rp 540.0 B** | Derecognizes dead suburban mall square meters |
| **Cr. Gain on Lease Modification** *(Book Profit)* | | **Rp 30.0 B** | Favorable termination gain recognized in P&L |

### Real Cash Flow Impact:
* Renegotiating 15 anchor mall hypermarket leases during 2021–2023 renewals saves **Rp 2.40 Billion per store per year** across 15 locations:
  $$15\text{ stores} \times Rp\ 2.40\text{B/yr} = \mathbf{Rp\ 36.0\text{ Billion/year in cash savings}}$$
* Over 5 years, this unlocks **+Rp 180.0 Billion in cumulative cash flow**, which directly finances the 50 compact Hyfresh store rollouts **with ZERO new bank debt**.
