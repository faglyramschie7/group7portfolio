Company background

PT Matahari Putra Prima Tbk (MPPA) is an Indonesian retailer headquartered in Tangerang. It operates Hypermart and a portfolio of supermarket, convenience, health and beauty, and wholesale formats.

Hypermart’s role

Hypermart contributed approximately 85% of MPPA’s revenue in 2020. Foodmart and Primo together contributed approximately 10%, while other formats contributed approximately 5%. These figures describe revenue contribution; they do not establish each format’s profitability.

The setting for the case

The case places MPPA amid COVID-19 restrictions, changing shopping behavior, and growing use of online retail. MPPA was developing its online channels while reviewing the role and size of its physical stores. The assignment asks for a strategic plan covering 2021–2025.

Source: case, printed pp. 587, 589–590, and 594. Choosing the preferred strategy belongs in the later analysis.

Section 03 · Vision and mission

Separate company statements from the authors’ illustration

The case reports that no vision or mission statement was posted on the company website or in the most recent annual report it reviewed. This is the textbook’s historical account, not an independently verified claim about the company today.

“To become the premier, multi-format, go-to shopping destination throughout Indonesia.”

This is the case authors’ suggested vision. The case supplies no corresponding mission statement in that section.

Initial assessment

The suggested vision identifies a retail business, Indonesia as the market, and a multi-format approach. It leaves the intended customers and distinctive reason to choose MPPA broad. We should assess those choices through the external and internal analyses before developing a proposed vision and a mission with the required components.

Section 04 · Statement of the problem

The problem and the decision

MPPA faces continuing operating losses and limited financial capacity while relying on Hypermart for approximately 85% of revenue. The strategic question is how it should allocate resources across store formats and online channels to achieve sustainable profitability.

Evidence behind the statement

Indicator20192020What it establishes
RevenueRp8,654.6bnRp6,746.6bnSales declined approximately 22%.
Operating result (EBIT)−Rp196.1bn−Rp140.3bnLosses continued, although the operating result improved.

Loss before taxRp314.6bnRp407.7bnThe operating improvement did not translate into a better pretax result.

Why the interest increase outweighed the operating improvement

Operating loss narrowed from Rp196.105bn to Rp140.268bn, an EBIT improvement of Rp55.837bn. Interest expense rose from Rp118.496bn to Rp267.419bn, an increase of Rp148.923bn. The additional interest exceeded the EBIT improvement by Rp93.086bn. Accordingly, pretax loss increased from Rp314.601bn to Rp407.687bn, also a deterioration of Rp93.086bn.

Reconciliation: Rp148.923bn additional interest − Rp55.837bn EBIT improvement = Rp93.086bn increase in pretax loss.

In 2019, current liabilities of Rp2,618.390bn exceeded current assets of Rp1,904.047bn by Rp714.343bn. The corresponding 2020 shortfall was Rp1,227.833bn, an increase of Rp513.490bn. Both years therefore had negative working capital, with a larger gap in 2020.

At year-end 2020, current assets were Rp1,535.266bn and current liabilities were Rp2,763.099bn. Their difference was −Rp1,227.833bn, or approximately −Rp1.23tn. This indicates constrained short-term financial capacity; it does not by itself establish when every payment is due.

Source: case, printed pp. 588–590, Exhibits 2, 3, and 5. Units in the original financial exhibits are millions of rupiah; this section converts them to billions and trillions.

Keep symptoms separate from explanations

Losses, declining sales, and the liquidity gap establish performance pressure. The case discusses pandemic restrictions and shopping behavior, but these do not establish all causes of the losses. MPPA was already recording an operating loss in 2019. Hypermart’s revenue concentration makes its performance important, without proving that concentration itself caused poor results.

Alternatives remain open

Improving existing Hypermart stores, selectively resizing or exiting locations, investing in smaller formats, and developing economically viable online services remain alternatives to assess. The problem statement does not assume which option will win. PESTLE is the next analysis.

Section 05 · External assessment

PESTLE analysis

This analysis uses external evidence published in 2020–2021, with older comparison periods identified. It supports the case’s 2021–2025 planning exercise. No 2022–2026 outcomes are used to select a strategy. Research checked on 4 November 2026.

How the textbook guides this analysis

Chapter 3 groups external forces into economic; social, cultural, demographic and environmental; political, governmental and legal; technological; and competitive forces. PESTLE separates some of these into the six headings requested for the presentation. Competitive forces require the later Five Forces and competitor analyses, so PESTLE alone is not a complete external assessment.

David’s AQCD test asks whether a factor helps inform action, includes quantities where possible, compares conditions across time, and relates to products, divisions or regions. The textbook also requires genuinely external facts, rather than proposed strategies presented as opportunities. The analysis below keeps the external evidence separate from interpretation and candidate responses.

What the evidence does and does not justify

The factors support comparing customer access, price and assortment choices, checkout convenience, compliance, and perishable inventory economics. They do not select a winning store format or prove that online expansion will be profitable. Company-level sales declines, debt and current liabilities belong to the internal assessment, rather than the PESTLE factor list.

Macro statistics are not divisional measurements. Mapping them to Hypermart, Hyfresh or a Jakarta location improves relevance but does not create missing format-level evidence. No AQCD score, EFE weight or response rating is assigned simply to fill the template.

Political and legal factors share a boundary. Pandemic operating policy is classified under Political; the specific shopping-bag obligation sits under Legal. Avoid counting the same condition twice when selecting factors for EFE.

Data comparisons and source reconciliation
  • Household consumption: 5.04% − (−2.63%) = 7.67 percentage points. Both are real growth rates, not nominal grocery spending.
  • USDA’s rounded channel-sales values imply convenience growth of approximately 6.8% and a hypermarket decline of approximately 13.0%. They are national USD sales, not customer-switching rates.
  • QRIS adoption compares end-2020 with 1 November 2021, not two complete calendar years. The later observation exceeds 12 million; the bar uses 12 as a labeled lower bound.
  • The Bappenas food-loss-and-waste range covers the national food chain across 2000–2019. No year-to-year change or company waste rate is inferred.
  • USDA describes MPPA’s 2020 online share as 4%; the textbook says 5%. Neither figure is used as an external PESTLE factor. Reconcile the underlying definitions before using the number in an internal model.
